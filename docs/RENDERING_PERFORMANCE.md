# Rendering performance and player visual preferences

## Reproducing the measurements

Use an imported editor/debug project and the Godot console executable. From the project directory run `python tools/render_benchmark.py --engine <executable> --cases probe,garden,combat,world --seconds 6`. The launcher creates a fresh marked profile and writes its location, engine log and JSON results. The driver refuses real storage and headless rendering. Select individual encounters with `--cases water,storm,spirit`, select `--size 1920x1080` or `2560x1440`, and use `--capture` for an explicit 8 Hz framebuffer readback, resize and JPEG write. `--cases isolate` measures CPU preparation and synchronized drawing separately. No driver cache is cleared.

All local results use the game camera, Godot 4.7.2 Compatibility/OpenGL, a GeForce RTX 5080 and Ryzen 7 9800X3D. The primary matrix uses 1100 × 720, VSync disabled. Each setup runs first at 120 FPS, repeats at 120, then runs uncapped. The process starts in the Developer Garden. “First” in the matrix means first use of that workload in that process. A separate launch is needed for a fresh process. Warm samples continue the same encounter. Each legal build uses three clustered targets, timed input requests and the existing practice lifecycle. Combat timing and target decisions can vary. The Grove, Mire and Hollow use depths 2, 10 and 16 and seeds 136, 137 and 129. The Grove is revisited in the same process.

Frame intervals include scheduling, simulation, interface, rendering and presentation waits. A frame above 50 ms is worth investigating even with a good p95. Each record includes sample size, median, p95, p99, p99.9 at 1,000+ frames, max and counts above 16.7/33.3/50/100 ms. Draws and primitives are the last periodic sample, effect counts are sampled peaks, and the engine's process monitor can still contain the preceding transition. Resource deltas are net counts. Neither a flat resource count nor a zero pipeline-counter delta proves that no allocations or driver work occurred. Timed scenario transitions include constructing the world and presenting it. The separately timed forced draw explicitly waits for the renderer.

## A. Diagnosis at VFX07B

The pre-optimization 1100 × 720 matrix exercised the starting Garden, developed Garden, 30 and 60 beds, ordinary strikes, Ember Wake, Avalanche ruptures, Monsoon, Conductor, combined Mirrorstep/Wraithcast, overlapping Void fields and a mixed build. The terrain trials used bridges where available, explored sight and real combat. The original layout hashes, observed effects and complete sample distributions accompany this report.

* At 30 beds the first six seconds after the fixture constructed the orchard had 11 frames above 50 ms, maximum 82.52 ms. The next capped pass had none and p99 was 8.70 ms. The initial developed Garden had one 72.24 ms frame. Bulk setup and steady Garden play must be reviewed separately. At 60 beds the warm uncapped p99 was 4.61 ms.
* Of the eight arena trials the first capped maxima ranged from 21.90 to 42.94 ms. The warm uncapped p99 ranged from 5.83 to 8.41 ms. This matrix did not reproduce a recurring active-combat frame above 50 ms. It does not explain the historical stalls.
* Generating a floor took 101–426 ms on the CPU. The first explicit Grove draw and sync took 24.49 ms and the repeat took 1.71 ms. A new floor view rebuilt identical family vegetation meshes in 5.78–7.77 ms. An immediate second lookup in that view reused the mesh. Re-entering the Grove rebuilt it in 6.93 ms. This identifies redundant CPU work on floor transitions.
* Previous measurements remain relevant. VFX03 had individual Garden frames around 31 ms. VFX05A had 46.92 ms with three burning targets. Monsoon reached 263.17 ms and fresh follow-ups roughly 33 ms. An uncaptured first-use Storm frame reached 310.41 ms and its repeat 10.24 ms. A Spirit recording frame reached 495.22 ms with framebuffer readback. Earlier forced first terrain draws reached 166.12 ms on VFX06B and 198.15 or 600.46 ms on VFX07, versus roughly 1.5–1.8 ms on repeats. The renderer, CPU, OS and capture contributions to these isolated frames remain unattributed. See [combat observations](COMBAT_READABILITY.md), [terrain draws](TERRAIN_GENERATION.md) and [Garden measurements](ARCHITECTURE.md).
* Isolating the 318 × 285 feature-distance texture took 41.21 ms for Grove, 75.91 ms for Mire and 15.74 ms for Hollow. Component measurements and texture byte digests allow a candidate to be checked for identical pixels independently of total floor time.
* A first Spirit release apparition took 4.26 ms to construct, 2.38 ms to seek through nine animation frames, and 154.05 ms in an explicitly synchronized full-scene draw. Nine repeats took 1.46–1.95 ms to construct and 1.39–2.05 ms to draw. An explicit repeat of all five numerical grip calibrations cost 0.33–0.51 ms per body. The sample supports reuse of immutable setup data, but does not attribute a historical 495 ms recording frame to skeleton setup or prove shader compilation. A separate warmed viewport sample reported 0.82 ms rendering CPU and 0.72 ms GPU. These timings cannot apportion the earlier full-scene wait.
* Two fresh, otherwise matched 1100 × 720 Spirit processes reached 54.77 ms without capture and 55.26 ms with capture on the first capped pass. Warm uncapped p99 increased from 6.68 to 11.61 ms with capture. The 1920 × 1080 Spirit pass and the higher-resolution standalone pass had warm uncapped p99 of 8.40 and 6.35 ms respectively. Windows constrained the requested 2560 × 1440 window to a measured 2560 × 1415. The 1080p workload followed Monsoon and Conductor, so its first Spirit sample has a different rendering history.
* [Original measurements](rendering-evidence/vfx07b.jsonl) contain one header per fresh process followed by the complete sample records. Timing in milliseconds. Threshold counts in ascending order.

## B. Measured optimization

Three changes target identified work. The terrain distance rasterizer allocates one feature's bounding rectangle and restricts each edge to the scanline band that can contribute; its original distance encoding and intersection math are preserved. The renderer retains the immutable mesh entries for three production botanical families across floors. Instance transforms, sight, climate, shader overrides and generated layouts remain floor-local. Traveller bodies reuse numerical grip calibration for the two imported skeletons. Each actor gets a deep copy of the mutable animation data and owns its own skeleton, player and effects. No prewarming, pooled actors or new effect limits were introduced.

| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 30 beds following construction | 77.80 → 104.96 | 8.70 → 8.56 | 4.06 → 3.34 |
| 60 beds | 8.56 → 8.67 | 8.68 → 8.60 | 4.61 → 3.63 |
| Sustained Fire | 9.48 → 9.33 | 9.50 → 9.30 | 6.64 → 5.22 |
| Conductor | 9.57 → 9.37 | 9.49 → 9.37 | 5.97 → 4.85 |
| Combined Spirit | 10.89 → 9.22 | 10.13 → 8.95 | 5.83 → 4.94 |
| Advanced mixed build | 10.26 → 9.61 | 10.10 → 9.91 | 8.41 → 5.55 |
| Mire combat | 9.46 → 9.30 | 9.36 → 9.22 | 8.24 → 5.27 |

These are paired configurations, not isolated causal estimates. Whole-game distributions also vary with scheduling, driver cache history and combat outcomes. The stronger causal evidence comes from the individual operations. Grove/Mire/Hollow texture builds went from 41.21/75.91/15.74 to 36.02/66.06/13.85 ms with the same pixel digest in every case. Repeat Grove floor preparation went from 173.18 to 142.55 ms, and the already built Grove mesh lookup took 1 µs. Warm median Spirit body construction fell from 1.50 to 0.89 ms. Both full matrices released each previous floor's atmosphere and previous practice world. All uncapped sample windows ended with **zero net change in resource count** on the candidate. Whole-process texture counters and screen geometry are in the sample records. The first synchronized Spirit draw was 154.05 ms originally and 31.04 ms in the candidate process; those numbers include driver waits and have no established causal attribution.

Fresh 1100 × 720 Spirit first-use maxima fell from 54.77 to 44.20 ms without recording and from 55.26 to 40.76 ms with recording. Warm uncapped p99 was 5.37 ms without recording and 9.70 ms with it. These repeats leave the older extreme spikes open.

The extra `--cases garden --seconds 3 --stalls` trace reproduced 11 consecutive slow frames immediately after ten scripted frontier purchases. Each one spent 59–70 ms before renderer submission, then 1.10–1.29 ms in the draw signals. Zero tweens were running. The disposable game's journal recorded growth-scene acknowledgments 8–19 at elapsed times 31612, 31686, 31757, 31821, 31884, 31946, 32010, 32073, 32134, 32197, 32257 and 32322 ms. Their spacing tracks the frame intervals. With cinematics disabled the existing Garden view commits one pending acknowledgment at a time. This establishes a main-thread backlog in the bulk-construction fixture; the cost of storage versus other pre-draw work within each frame is unmeasured. Optimizing it would require attention to the acknowledgment/transaction owner. The subsequent warm Garden had no comparable burst. The primary candidate's longest frame was 126.26 ms following bulk construction.

The [candidate measurements](rendering-evidence/candidate.jsonl) were taken on the working candidate over checkpoint A. Focused weapon presentation, Spirit, terrain features, botanical art and terrain continuity fixtures passed, including comparison of calibrated actors with independent mutable playback state.

The short-profile probe runs both processes against a newly marked OS-temporary profile. No machine or driver cache is removed.
* The focused planner still reports the two original saved Garden layout mismatches, scopes 3 and 15. Their historical expectations are preserved. Cooperative Developer Testing Arena remains deferred at its session-ownership boundary. The reliable workflow here uses ordinary multiplayer fixtures.
* The ordinary guest expedition-summary and checkpoint card-offer timeouts remain open. No network or settlement change is part of these checkpoints. The earlier Monsoon, Storm, recording and synchronous terrain draw outliers still lack a definitive cause. Separate-PC, internet, lower-end and non-OpenGL rendering require further testing.


### Visual comparison and functional verification

The charge outline, chevrons, target end, three enemy silhouettes, fog boundary and floor HUD are visible in both the [Low capture](rendering-evidence/mire-low.png) and the [High capture](rendering-evidence/mire-high.png). Low removes cast shadows; the trees cast shadows across the path on High. The tree at the far end partially overlaps the charge outline at both levels. The charge vertices and generated layout stayed identical through all five preset switches. This single scene establishes a reference for human review in other terrain and against other attack shapes.

The fresh Low and High Spirit trials included viewport timer sampling. On the last uncapped draw, Low used 549 calls and High 737. The sampled median viewport GPU times were 0.35 and 0.50 ms. Warm uncapped **whole-frame** p99 was 6.80 and 5.04 ms respectively. Low had a 94.93 ms first-pass frame versus 48.63 ms on High. Fewer draws did not translate to a consistent frame-time ranking on this machine. Low, High and Custom each survived a separate-process settings reload on a short marked Windows profile. ### Ordinary multiplayer on one computer

Each ordinary ENet client had its own marked save root and an 800 × 520 window capped at 60 FPS. The host used High and guests used Low, Medium and Ultra. The graphical test driver uses the fixtures' keyboard attacks and facing, and ignores the single shared desktop pointer. The actual action requests, combat admission, hit detection, snapshot transport, local rendering and settlement paths ran normally. The fifth connection tested overflow refusal.

* Two rendered participants completed Spirit, Wood, Vital and Water encounters. Clients rendered returning waves and healing, protection, and Spirit manifestations. The four-player mixed encounter completed native Storm, Spirit and Void combat, a shared Frost rupture, a full enemy snapshot and fifth-player refusal. Four-player Water also completed independent tides, card selection, floor changes and cleanup. A separate four-player flora run exercised fog, harvestable specimens, Returning Roots, construction, rejoin and resources secured with different local graphics settings.
* In the successful mixed run, p99 for frames ending with active effects was 28.55 ms on the host and 18.04–18.17 ms on guests. Individual such frames reached 301.90 ms on the host and 221.71–260.38 ms on guests. In the successful Water party, those p99s were 95.94 ms and 44.18–51.59 ms. A frame can contain synchronous fixture or lifecycle work while a previous visual still exists. This measurement cannot assign the entire interval to the GPU or to that effect.
* Across all phases the mixed host reached 891.90 ms. A four-player flora host recorded a 4.14 s interval in a fixture that performs bulk construction, save transactions, scene transitions and rejoin on the same computer. These figures are important for interpreting the fixture and carry no separate-machine latency claim.
* Early graphical attempts were sensitive to all four windows receiving the same physical pointer. One guest also missed the existing requirement for a current snapshot within 500 ms. The previously reported checkpoint-card offer failure recurred on an earlier Spirit pair and on a headless Water party. All original failures and retries remain in the [ordinary multiplayer evidence](rendering-evidence/multiplayer.jsonl). The final driver uses the ordinary keyboard-input fixtures without changing their assertions or deadlines.

A follow-up recorded the five worst frame intervals per process. In the four-player flora run the 4.41 s host interval ended with four participants and zero combat effects or warning shapes. In the mixed party, two host intervals of 928 and 739 ms ended with zero effects. A separate 440 ms host frame ended with seven active hurt/pulse effects and six warnings. These observations separate periods with and without active effects; they do not identify the cause of the 440 ms frame. On a Water repeat, the guest checkpoint card-offer timeout also exposed a fixture teardown error when two waiting guests inspected a freed world. The test now detects a closed world while retaining the original gate condition and timeout. The ordinary card service and its timeout remain under investigation separately.

The Water timeout reproduced again after the test teardown correction. The guests reported the missing card offer and gate completion normally, with no freed-object engine errors. Preserve both this failure and the earlier successful four-player Water run. A successful retry alone would not establish a fix for the card-offer timeout.
